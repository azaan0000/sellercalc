Quick answer

Net 30 means payment is due 30 days after the invoice date — an invoice dated October 22, 2026 is due November 21, 2026. With EOM terms, the clock starts on the last day of the invoice month instead of the invoice date.

How the net payment terms due date works

Due date = Invoice date + Net days  ·  With EOM: Due date = last day of invoice month + Net days

Trade credit is written as 'Net N': Net 15, Net 30, Net 45, Net 60, Net 75 and Net 90 are the common ladder. The number is calendar days, not business days. '2/10 Net 30' adds an early-payment discount (2% if paid within 10 days). 'EOM' (end of month) shifts the start of the clock to the month's end — which quietly adds up to a month of extra credit for invoices sent early in a month.

Worked example

Invoice dated October 22 on Net 45 EOM terms: the clock starts November 1, so payment is due December 16, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What does Net 30 EOM mean?

The buyer gets 30 days starting from the END of the invoice month. An invoice sent October 5 on Net 30 EOM is due December 1, not November 4 — the EOM rule adds roughly the remainder of the sending month.

Do Net terms count weekends and holidays?

Yes. Net N means N calendar days from the invoice date. If the due date lands on a weekend or bank holiday, payment is typically expected the next business day — or before it, depending on the contract.

What are standard payment terms for freelancers and wholesalers?

Net 15 and Net 30 are the most common for small business and freelancers. Net 60–90 usually signals large corporate buyers with slow accounts-payable cycles — price the waiting time into your rates.
