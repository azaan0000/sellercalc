Quick answer

Break-even ROAS is the ad return your store must hit before it starts losing money. It equals selling price ÷ (price − product cost − fees per unit). A $30 product with $18 of total unit costs needs a 2.5 ROAS just to break even — every point above that is profit.

How the break-even roas works

Break-even ROAS = Price ÷ (Price − Cost − Fees)  ·  Target ROAS = Price ÷ (Price − Cost − Fees − Price × Margin%)

Your ad platform reports ROAS (return on ad spend) as revenue ÷ ad cost. But revenue is not profit: every order also carries product, shipping and payment costs. The ROAS at which revenue exactly covers all costs is your break-even ROAS. Below it, scaling ads just scales losses.

Worked example

$30 product · $12 landed cost · $3 fees → contribution is $15, so break-even ROAS = 30 ÷ 15 = 2.0. For a 15% net margin ($4.50 profit per unit) your target ROAS is 30 ÷ (15 − 4.50) = 2.86.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good ROAS for e-commerce?

Most healthy stores need a ROAS above roughly 2–3 just to break even, because product and shipping consume 50–70% of revenue. A 'good' ROAS is any value clearly above your break-even number — find yours with this calculator before judging campaign performance.

Why does break-even ROAS rise when margins are thin?

When (price − cost − fees) is small, each dollar of revenue carries almost no profit, so ads must return many dollars for every dollar spent. As contribution margin approaches zero, break-even ROAS approaches infinity.

Does this work for Amazon FBA and Shopify?

Yes. For FBA include the item price, Amazon referral + FBA fulfilment fees in 'fees', and ad spend per unit if you spread it per order. For Shopify include product cost, shipping, payment processing and app fees.
