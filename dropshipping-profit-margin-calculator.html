Quick answer

Dropshipping profit per order is selling price minus product cost (including supplier shipping), payment and platform fees, and ad spend per order. A $35 product with $14 supplier cost, 5% fees and $8 of ads nets about $11.25 per order — a 32% margin.

How the dropshipping profit margin works

Profit = Price − (Supplier cost + Price × Fee% + Ad spend per order)  ·  Net margin = Profit ÷ Price × 100

Most dropshipping failures are arithmetic failures: the store looked busy but every order quietly lost a few dollars. Platforms show revenue, not landed cost. Count four things per order: product + shipping cost from your supplier (CJ, AliExpress or Zendrop), payment processing and platform fees, ad spend per order, and returns/refunds (rough them in or add ~2–3% to fees).

Worked example

$35 price · $14 supplier cost · 5% fees ($1.75) · $8 ads → total cost $23.75, profit $11.25 (32% margin). Profit per 100 orders: $1,125.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good profit margin for dropshipping?

Healthy dropshipping margins are typically 15–30% net after ad spend. Below 10%, a single price hike from the supplier or a bad ad week erases profit. Above 30% is excellent and worth scaling.

Should ad spend be counted per order?

Yes — divide weekly ad cost by weekly orders. If you spend $700 and get 87 orders, that is about $8 per order. Ignoring it is the #1 reason stores think they are profitable when they are not.

What costs does this calculator miss?

It assumes a fulfilled order. Refunds, chargebacks and supplier re-ship costs are not included — add 2–3% to the fee field to budget for them.
