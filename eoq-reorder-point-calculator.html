Economic order quantity and reorder point from demand, order cost, holding cost and lead time.

Quick answer

EOQ = √(2 × annual demand × cost per order ÷ holding cost per unit). For 5,000 units/year, $25 per order and $3 holding cost per unit, the economic order quantity is about 289 units. Reorder point = average daily sales × supplier lead time + safety stock.

How the eoq & reorder point works

EOQ = √(2 × D × S ÷ H)  ·  Reorder point = Daily demand × Lead time + Safety stock

Order too much and you pay storage and tie up cash; order too little and you pay shipping over and over. The EOQ formula finds the order size where the two costs balance. The reorder point tells you when to place the order so stock arrives before you run out: average daily sales × supplier lead time, plus a safety buffer for late shipments and demand spikes.

Worked example

5,000 units/year, $25 per order, $3 holding → EOQ = √(2·5000·25/3) ≈ 289 units, about 17 orders a year. Selling 20/day with a 14-day lead time and 30 units of safety stock: reorder when inventory hits 310 units.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good holding cost estimate?

For small e-commerce sellers, holding cost is usually 15–25% of the unit's purchase price per year (storage, capital, insurance, shrinkage). A $15 product costs roughly $2.25–$3.75 per year to keep.

How do I choose safety stock?

Start with 1–2 weeks of average sales. If your supplier or sales are erratic, cover the worst recent lead-time delay: safety stock ≈ (longest lead time − average lead time) × daily sales.

Should dropshippers use EOQ?

Classic EOQ assumes you hold stock, so pure dropshippers use just the reorder-point half to time supplier pre-buys. Sellers who bulk-import from CJ or AliExpress into a local warehouse use the full model.
